nine destinations here, three rings on the map — where the other six went

The map covers the 44 landing pages, which this audit defined as sitemap handles ending in -lp. Six advertised destinations fall outside that net, and each one is worth naming:

Two are product pages. teens-kickstart-vitamin carries 10 ad links — more than any landing page. The biggest paid destination on the whole store is a PDP, not an LP.
Three are pages whose handle has no -lp suffix — including 5-reasons-pediatricians-recommend-first-day, which is in the sitemap and is plainly a landing page. The suffix is a naming convention, not a definition, and this is where that shortcut costs coverage.
One is on another host. reviews.firstday.com is a separate property outside this crawl entirely.

And one advertised page, kde-bfcm-behind-the-science-lp, does not appear in the supplied sitemap at all — live paid traffic pointing at a page their own sitemap does not list.

The catalogue, including its shadow copy

why “Test” is not a guess

Test is a value in First Day's own product_type field — one of twelve they use, alongside Full Price, Retail, Refill Pouch, Bundle, Gift, Sample and Discontinued. It is read verbatim from products.json; no keyword matching, no inference from titles.

Three things corroborate it. Every one duplicates a live SKU. Every price is exactly 1.19× its live twin — $39.00 → $46.42 and $49.00 → $58.32, the same multiplier across all 21. And one is titled “Kids' Daily Focus (Test Copy)”. A uniform multiplier over a duplicated catalogue is a price test.

What this does not establish: whether the test is still running, or whether these are meant to be publicly listed. They are reachable today on the public products.json, which is the only claim made here.

sourceShopify’s own products.json, which the store serves publicly. Prices, types, variants and publish dates are the store’s own fields, unmodified.
