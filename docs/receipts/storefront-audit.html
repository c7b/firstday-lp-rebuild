Independent storefront audit · August 2026

What the storefront looks like from outside it

Everything below was measured from sources First Day already publishes to anyone — the Shopify JSON routes the store serves, its own sitemap, the HTML of its live pages, and Meta's public ad library. No account access, no credentials, nothing a competitor could not also see.

Why this exists. The brief asked for one landing page rebuilt. Before proposing where a rebuild should go next, it seemed more useful to measure what is actually there — and a few of the findings turned out to be things worth knowing regardless of who does the work.

Finding 01 — Most landing pages are locked inside a page builder

Measured across 44 landing pages

32 of 44 have no Shopify sections at all

They are Replo exports: the entire page body is markup produced by an external page builder and pasted in. Only 12 are built from Shopify sections. The practical consequence is that for roughly three quarters of the landing pages, nobody on the team can change a headline without going through the page builder — not in the theme editor, not in Shopify at all.

This also explains the weight. The median landing page ships 2.1 MB of HTML and loads 45 scripts before anyone sees a product.

Finding 03 — Twenty-six tools load on every page

Fingerprinted across 45 pages

Reviews, clinician reviews, email, SMS, price testing, attribution, support, subscriptions, a page builder, translation, identity resolution, server-side tracking, session replay, tag management, affiliates, donations, consent, package protection, post-purchase upsell — plus three ad pixels.

Every one of them was found on 100% of the pages measured, including pages where the feature is not used. Two roles have two vendors doing the same job.

Not one of these is wrong on its own. The number that matters is the one at the top of the page: 26 on every page, 45 scripts on a median landing page. An audit of which ones still earn their slot is the cheapest performance work available, and it needs a business decision rather than an engineer.

Finding 04 — The catalogue has a shadow copy

Shopify products.json · public

100 published products, 138 variants — and 22 of them are typed “Test”

Those 22 look like price-test SKUs, which is consistent with the store running Intelligems for price testing, so they are almost certainly deliberate. Worth knowing anyway: they are publicly reachable and publicly listed, priced differently from the products they duplicate ($46.42 against $39.00 on the equivalent SKU). Duplicate, indexable product pages are a search-visibility question more than an engineering one.

Finding 05 — Content is published faster than it is retired

Sitemap lastmod

87 of 383 URLs have not been touched since 2024

Meanwhile five landing pages were modified in the last ten days — including the one the brief handed over (23 July) and its Kids sibling (29 July). The estate is simultaneously very active at the front and very stale at the back. Both ends cost something: the stale end is crawl budget and search dilution, the active end is where the duplication multiplies.

Finding 06 — The site is already positioning for AI agents

Sitemap · agentic discovery

firstday.com publishes an agents.md

The sitemap carries an agentic-discovery entry — the store is declaring itself to AI agents. That is ahead of most DTC brands, and it makes one thing worth checking: an agent reading that file needs the funnel URLs and product data to be accurate and current. Right now the landing-page estate has pages the sitemap does not list, and product duplicates that a machine reader cannot tell apart from the real thing.

Method and limits. Sources: Shopify's public products.json and collections.json, the sitemap supplied with the brief, the HTML of 44 landing pages fetched once each with a delay between requests, and Meta's public Ad Library (active, United States). Lighthouse figures are local runs with mobile emulation, identical settings on both pages. Vendor detection is fingerprint-based: it proves a script is present, not that a feature is switched on. Ad counts are link occurrences, not spend. Product typing is read from the store's own fields. Nothing here used privileged access, and everything is reproducible from the tools in the repository this report ships with.
